\section{Recommendations, Transfer and Limitations}\label{sec:recs}

\textbf{Recommendations.} (1)~Improve splitting and value reading: \plWrongRuleS{} of the \plWrongS{} wrong sentences had every clause label right, so a better classifier cannot fix most of them. (2)~Before any release, run the pilot the proposal planned with real students, measuring condition accuracy per group, corrections made, time to choose a timetable, and how often nothing fits. (3)~Treat the learned ranker as unproven: with \sRankExamPersonas{} exam personas its paired interval against equal weights spanned \rDiffEqLo{} to \rDiffEqHi{} points; a rough calculation gives about \rPilotStudents{} students for 80\% power to detect a \rDiffEq-point gain, assuming variability like this synthetic exam (Appendix~\ref{app:model}), to be recalculated from real-student pilot data, and the ranker should be kept only if its interval is above zero. (4)~Keep asking back: it covered most clause-label errors at about \cPerSentence{} questions or offers per sentence.

\textbf{Transfer to engineering scheduling.} The same structure fits wherever requirements are written in words and a schedule must satisfy hard constraints. A planner's note such as ``line 2 is down on Tuesday morning; keep changeovers low this week'' would become machine availability (hard) and changeover cost (soft); a time-limited solver would replace exact enumeration, as a factory has too many schedules to list~\cite{pinedo2022}; and the ranker would learn from the plans planners chose; none of this is tested. Misreads cost more there, so confirmation matters more.

\textbf{Limitations.}\label{sec:limits} No real student took part: all sentences, personas, labels and exams were AI-written, so the results show the method works on AI-written students, not that it helps real ones. The two exam annotators agreed on every parsing label, but they were the same model reading the same rules, and the exam writer removed \sExamDropped{} sentences the rules could not settle, so real sentences will be harder; Claude also wrote part of the training data and every persona, so the exams may share its style. The data covers one semester and \dUnits{} units, and the proposal's usability targets were not measured.
